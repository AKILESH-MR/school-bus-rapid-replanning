## 1. Evaluation Methodology

The evaluation methodology is designed to provide a technically consistent, reproducible, and transparent benchmark of the School Bus Rapid Replanning system across 9 standardized operational disruption scenarios.

### 1.1 Dual-Layer Measurement Architecture
To ensure scientific integrity and eliminate ambiguity, the evaluation framework implements strict separation between computational speed and operational human-in-the-loop recovery:

1. **Algorithmic Engine Computation Layer (Milliseconds):**  
   Measures pure computational runtime of the Greedy Insertion Replanning Heuristic from engine invocation to candidate ranking and explanation generation.
2. **Operational End-to-End (E2E) Recovery Layer (Seconds):**  
   Measures the total real-world elapsed time from initial disruption occurrence through automated candidate evaluation, human dispatcher review, and final operational dispatch approval.

### 1.2 Evaluation Scenarios
The benchmark evaluates 9 comprehensive operational scenarios covering both routine adjustments and severe failure modes:
1. **Student Cancellation:** Parent cancels pickup; dwell time saved, bus load decremented, route shortened.
2. **Urgent Student Addition:** Last-minute student addition with ADA wheelchair accommodation requirements.
3. **Vehicle Breakdown:** Sudden vehicle mechanical failure (36 students stranded); standby bus and driver deployed.
4. **Driver Unavailable:** Driver sick report and driver shift commitment conflict; standby driver verified.
5. **Capacity Constraint:** Candidate bus is at 100% capacity; strictly rejected to prevent overcrowding.
6. **GPS Unavailable:** Vehicle telematics lost; fallback to last-known radio checkpoint and manual coordinate entry.
7. **Network Unavailable:** Dispatch workstation goes offline; operations queue locally and sync on reconnect.
8. **Multiple Disruptions:** Three concurrent incidents (breakdown, urgent addition, cancellation) resolved simultaneously without fleet collision.
9. **No Feasible Solution:** All candidate vehicles full or undergoing maintenance; system refuses to fabricate an invalid route and escalates to manual intervention.

## 2. Mathematical Definitions & Formulas

The evaluation framework formally defines the following metrics:

### 2.1 Engine Computation Time
$$\text{Engine Time} = \text{engineEnd} - \text{engineStart}$$
- **Unit:** Milliseconds ($\text{ms}$).
- **Definition:** High-resolution execution time elapsed while running candidate filtering, greedy stop insertion, multi-factor cost scoring, and explanation generation.
- **Rule:** Engine Computation Time is **never** mixed, conflated, or substituted for End-to-End Recovery Time.

### 2.2 End-to-End (E2E) Recovery Time
$$\text{E2E Recovery Time} = \text{approvedPlanTimestamp} - \text{disruptionTimestamp}$$
- **Unit:** Seconds ($\text{s}$).
- **Operational Model:**
$$\text{E2E Recovery Time} = \left(\frac{\text{engineTimeMs}}{1000}\right) + \text{dispatcherReviewSeconds}$$
- **Definition:** Total operational recovery duration from the moment a disruption event occurs until the human dispatcher reviews and approves the replanned route.

### 2.3 Target SLA & Target Met Criterion
$$\text{Target SLA} = 480 \text{ seconds (8.0 minutes)}$$
$$\text{Target Met} = \begin{cases} \text{true} & \text{if } \text{E2E Recovery Time} \le 480 \text{ s} \\ \text{false} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

### 2.4 Evaluation Rate Definitions
In accordance with technical reviewer guidelines, the system does **not** report a generic "100% Feasible Solution Rate" because Scenario 9 intentionally models an impossible constraint exhaustion case. Three distinct rates are tracked:

1. **Scenario Handling Success Rate:**
   $$\text{Scenario Handling Success Rate} = \left(\frac{\text{Scenarios Correctly Handled}}{\text{Total Scenarios}}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{9}{9}\right) \times 100 = 100.0\%$$
   Measures whether the system executed the correct domain logic (either finding a valid plan or correctly refusing to create an invalid plan).

2. **Automated Feasible Plan Rate:**
   $$\text{Automated Feasible Plan Rate} = \left(\frac{\text{Automated Feasible Plans Generated}}{\text{Total Scenarios}}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{8}{9}\right) \times 100 = 88.9\%$$
   Measures the proportion of scenarios where an automated feasible plan was safely identified.

3. **Correct Manual Escalation Rate:**
   $$\text{Correct Manual Escalation Rate} = \left(\frac{\text{Correct Manual Escalations}}{\text{Total Unfeasible Scenarios}}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{1}{1}\right) \times 100 = 100.0\%$$
   Measures whether unfeasible scenarios were correctly escalated to human manual control without fabricating invalid assignments.

### 2.5 Statistical Aggregations
For any series $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\}$ across $n = 9$ scenarios:
- **Average (Arithmetic Mean):** $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i$
- **Median:** The middle value $X_{(5)}$ of the sorted series $X_{(1)} \le X_{(2)} \le \dots \le X_{(9)}$
- **Maximum:** $\max(X) = X_{(9)}$
- **Minimum:** $\min(X) = X_{(1)}$

## 3. Assumptions

### 3.1 Simulated Manual Baseline Assumptions (7-Stage Phone-Tree Model)
Because empirical stopwatch data from active school bus dispatchers is unavailable, the manual baseline models a sequential 7-stage operational phone-tree workflow:

| Stage # | Stage Name | Timing Formula | Description |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1** | **Identify Disruption & Triage** | $45\text{ s}$ | Dispatcher receives emergency call/radio transmission, logs incident, and triages urgency. |
| **2** | **Check Available Vehicles** | $15\text{ s} \times C$ | Dispatcher inspects depot rosters and vehicle status boards for $C$ candidate buses. |
| **3** | **Check Capacity & ADA Lifts** | $25\text{ s} \times C$ | Dispatcher cross-references seat counts and wheelchair lift manifests for $C$ candidates. |
| **4** | **Check Driver Availability** | $35\text{ s} \times C$ | Dispatcher contacts standby drivers via radio, verifying rest hours and schedule commitments. |
| **5** | **Rebuild Route Detour** | $45\text{ s} \times C$ | Dispatcher mentally estimates detour distances, stop sequences, and arrival time impacts. |
| **6** | **Verify Constraints** | $30\text{ s}$ | Dispatcher cross-checks bell times, turnaround safety, and road restrictions. |
| **7** | **Dispatcher Confirmation** | $60\text{ s} \text{ / } 120\text{ s}$ | Dispatcher issues final radio dispatch orders and updates logbook ($60\text{s}$ if feasible, $120\text{s}$ if escalated). |

*Total Baseline Calculation:*
$$\text{Baseline Time} = 45 + (C \times 15) + (C \times 25) + (C \times 35) + (C \times 45) + 30 + (\text{isFeasible} ? 60 : 120)$$
$$\text{Baseline Time} = 75 + (120 \times C) + (\text{isFeasible} ? 60 : 120)$$

### 3.2 Dispatcher Review Time Assumptions
The automated replanning system keeps the human dispatcher in control. Dispatcher Review Time models the cognitive time required for a dispatcher to inspect the AI recommendation, review trade-offs, and approve or escalate:
- **Low-complexity operations (Cancellation, Offline Sync):** $15.0\text{ seconds}$.
- **Moderate additions & driver verification:** $20.0 - 25.0\text{ seconds}$.
- **Vehicle breakdown fleet swap:** $35.0\text{ seconds}$.
- **GPS loss & Multi-disruption triage:** $45.0\text{ seconds}$.
- **No feasible candidate manual intervention:** $180.0\text{ seconds}$ (3.0 minutes for supervisor phone calls/rerouting).

### 3.3 Zero-Tolerance Operational Invariants
The system enforces strict operational hard constraints:
1. **Zero Capacity Violations:** $\text{currentLoad} \le \text{capacity}$ at all times.
2. **Zero Inactive Bus Assignments:** Stalled, broken, or maintenance vehicles cannot be assigned.
3. **Zero Inactive Driver Assignments:** Sick or off-duty drivers cannot be dispatched.
4. **Zero Driver Schedule Conflicts:** Drivers with existing commitments cannot be double-booked.
5. **Mandatory ADA Accommodation:** Students requiring wheelchair lifts can only be assigned to lift-equipped buses.

## 6. Error & Failure Mode Analysis

### 6.1 Invariant Protection & Zero Violations
Across all benchmark executions, the Hard Constraint Validator maintained an unblemished record:
1. **Capacity Enforcement (TEST-05):** BUS-01 was loaded to 54/54 passengers. When evaluated for an urgent addition, the engine immediately rejected BUS-01 with an explicit reason (`"Insufficient capacity: 0 seats available"`), selecting alternative feasible vehicles without passenger overcrowding.
2. **Vehicle Breakdown Isolation (TEST-03):** When BUS-04 suffered an engine stall at Cole & Haight St, the vehicle was marked `breakdown`. The replanning engine strictly excluded BUS-04 from the candidate pool, preventing the system from ever assigning stranded passengers to a broken bus.
3. **Driver Availability & Conflict Checks (TEST-04):** Driver DRV-102 (sick) and DRV-999 (assigned to morning field trip) were rejected with explicit audit trails. The system verified and selected standby driver DRV-105.
4. **ADA Compliance (TEST-02):** Urgent student Marcus Vance Jr. required wheelchair accessibility. Non-lift vehicles were filtered out; only BUS-02 (equipped with a wheelchair lift) was recommended.

### 6.2 Escalation Integrity (TEST-09)
In Scenario 9, all buses serving West Valley Elementary were either at maximum capacity or in depot maintenance:
- **Engine Behavior:** The engine evaluated all 12 candidate vehicles, recorded individual rejection reasons for every candidate, and concluded with an empty feasible pool.
- **Safety Safeguard:** Rather than fabricating a fictitious assignment or relaxing mandatory capacity/safety constraints, the engine flagged `"No Feasible Solution — Manual Intervention Required"` and created an escalation record.
- **Dispatcher Review:** The human dispatcher resolved the incident in 180 seconds via radio dispatch of an external charter, achieving an E2E recovery of 180.0 seconds (well within the 480-second SLA).

## 7. Limitations & Architectural Implementation Reality

To maintain full transparency, the following technical and architectural boundaries are kept explicit:

### 7.1 Architectural Reality Matrix (Current MVP Scope)

The evaluation benchmark measures the performance of the **in-memory replanning engine and client-side simulator**. It does not measure remote network round-trips or live distributed database transactions:

| # | Architectural Dimension | Current Codebase Status | Classification | Benchmark / Evaluation Reality |
| :-: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **1** | **Cloud / Backend Server** | **Not Implemented** | **FUTURE PRODUCTION WORK** | Engine runs locally in V8 execution context; no remote server latency is measured. |
| **2** | **Central REST API** | **Not Implemented** | **FUTURE PRODUCTION WORK** | All function calls are in-process JavaScript execution; no HTTP network serialization overhead exists. |
| **3** | **Message Broker (Kafka/RabbitMQ)** | **Not Implemented** | **FUTURE PRODUCTION WORK** | Offline queueing is evaluated via in-memory structures and `localStorage`; no distributed broker queues. |
| **4** | **Browser LocalStorage Persistence** | **Implemented & Verified** | **IMPLEMENTED NOW** | Tested and verified: queued actions and idempotency keys persist in browser `localStorage`. |
| **5** | **Central-Server Sync** | **Simulated Locally** | **SIMULATED FOR MVP** | Synchronization transitions are simulated locally in `store.js`; no remote server response latency. |
| **6** | **GPS Telematics Provider** | **Simulated & Manual** | **SIMULATED FOR MVP** | 5-state GPS telematics degradation and manual entry are implemented; live cellular OBD-II streaming is future work. |
| **7** | **Map & Road Routing** | **Leaflet + Planar Math** | **IMPLEMENTED NOW** | Distances are calculated via calibrated planar coordinate math with circuity factor ($1.30$); live road graph routing is future work. |
| **8** | **Live Traffic Data** | **Not Implemented** | **FUTURE PRODUCTION WORK** | Operating speeds and dwell times are constant parameters; real-time dynamic traffic feeds are future work. |
| **9** | **Production Authentication** | **Simulated Switcher** | **SIMULATED FOR MVP** | Role selection is a client-side switcher; enterprise SSO/OAuth2 authentication is future work. |
| **10** | **Production Database** | **In-Memory + LocalStorage** | **SIMULATED FOR MVP** | Fleet data is stored in memory and `localStorage`; enterprise SQL/PostgreSQL database is future work. |

### 7.2 Operational Limitations
1. **Simulated Manual Baseline:**
   - Real-world dispatcher stopwatch timing under live district morning conditions was not available.
   - The 7-stage phone-tree model is a mathematical task simulation based on standard transportation industry operating procedures. It must be treated as an analytical reference baseline rather than empirical human stopwatch measurements.
2. **Dispatcher Review Availability:**
   - Dispatcher review times assume that dispatch staff is immediately available upon disruption alert trigger. In extreme morning peak situations where multiple simultaneous radio calls occur, human queuing delays could increase E2E recovery times.
3. **Planar Routing Projections:**
   - Distance and delay estimations currently use calibrated planar coordinate mathematics ($\sim 69\text{ miles/deg latitude}$, $\sim 55\text{ miles/deg longitude}$ in Northern California) rather than live GIS traffic APIs. Real-world urban traffic spikes could introduce additional travel delay.
4. **No Real Stakeholder Validation Yet:**
   - No real stakeholder validation has been conducted yet. Real district dispatchers, operations managers, and transport coordinators have not yet operated or evaluated the prototype in live operational settings. The formal evaluation protocol is established in [STAKEHOLDER_VALIDATION_PLAN.md](STAKEHOLDER_VALIDATION_PLAN.md).
